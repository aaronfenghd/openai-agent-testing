# Model Assumptions in Oncology CEA

## Purpose
Assumptions connect incomplete evidence to a decision-usable model. They should be transparent, justified, and tested where uncertainty is decision-relevant.

## Key assumption domains
### Proportional hazards (PH)
PH may be appropriate when hazard ratio behavior is clinically plausible and supported by evidence. It should be reassessed if survival curves suggest non-proportional effects.

### Constant hazard ratio after a time point
This may be considered for extrapolation stability but requires justification and should be tested in scenario analysis if uncertain.

### Treatment effect waning
Waning should be considered when sustained long-term benefit is uncertain. Timing and magnitude require justification and sensitivity testing.

### Cure and mixture-cure assumptions
Cure frameworks may be appropriate when long-term remission or survival plateau is clinically plausible. They should not be used only because statistical fit appears favorable. They usually require clinical plausibility, longer follow-up, external data, or expert validation. If evidence is uncertain, cure assumptions may be better positioned as scenario analyses.

### Mortality constraints
Long-term mortality assumptions should be justified. Mortality for long-term survivors should generally not be implausibly lower than general population mortality without strong rationale.

### Treatment duration and relative dose intensity
Duration and dose intensity assumptions affect drug costs and outcomes and should align with trial protocol, observed use, or expected practice patterns.

### Subsequent therapy assumptions
Post-progression regimens, treatment mix, and effectiveness assumptions may be highly influential and should be explicitly documented.

### Adverse event timing assumptions
Timing, duration, and recurrence assumptions for adverse events should be aligned to evidence and tested where uncertain.

### Utility and cost assumptions
Utility mapping, health-state assignment, and cost inclusion choices should be explicit, with source hierarchy and uncertainty handling defined.

## Documentation expectations
- State each assumption clearly.
- Label whether evidence-based, expert-elicited, or structural.
- Record rationale and expected direction of impact.
- Mark uncertain items as to be confirmed based on available evidence.

## Scenario testing expectations
Assumptions with material impact should be assessed in scenario analysis if uncertain, including waning, cure, mortality constraints, subsequent therapy, and utility/cost structural choices.

## Common HTA / ERG / EAG concerns
- Overly optimistic long-term treatment effects.
- Cure assumptions without sufficient plausibility support.
- Incomplete treatment duration or subsequent therapy handling.
- Utility/cost assumptions not aligned with perspective or evidence.

## Questions the HE MDP Agent should ask
- Which assumptions are supported directly by evidence?
- Which assumptions are structural placeholders pending evidence?
- Which assumptions are expected to be decision-sensitive?
- Which assumptions need scenario testing in the base analysis plan?

## MDP implications
Assumption governance should be part of the MDP: assumption log, rationale, status, and prespecified sensitivity/scenario tests.

## Example wording
"Key structural assumptions include treatment effect waning, post-progression therapy mix, and long-term survival constraints. These assumptions require justification based on available evidence and clinical input, and should be assessed in scenario analysis where uncertainty is material."
